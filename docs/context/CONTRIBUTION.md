# PharmaGuard: Verified Project Contributions

PharmaGuard demonstrates that grounding a large language model in deterministic, multi-source biomedical tools (openFDA FAERS disproportionality statistics, ChEMBL target biological mechanisms, and version-rubric PubMed literature grading) measurably eliminates hallucinated clinical confidence, enforces hard safety gating, and reduces clinician over-caution alert fatigue compared to an ungrounded LLM answering from memory alone. Rather than presenting unexamined claims of perfect recall, this project contributes an empirically audited, reproducible benchmark and methodology that exposes, quantifies, and isolates the failure modes of generative clinical AI in postmarketing pharmacovigilance.

### Grounded Project Contributions:

- **Epistemic Calibration Over Indiscriminate Recall:** The pipeline achieves 1.000 Strict Precision [0.610–1.000] with zero false-positive escalations on negative controls and an honest 0.857 (6/7) Strict Recall [0.487–0.974]. The apparent "15/15" recall of ungrounded models is rejected because it stems from indiscriminate over-escalation; the one strict false negative (*montelukast::suicidal_ideation*) is properly routed to `MONITOR` (confidence 0.664) due to unconfirmed CNS receptor mechanisms, a decision preserved when an attempted post-hoc rubric revision was identified as subjective bias and reverted ([`DECISIONS.md §15`](DECISIONS.md#L150)).
- **Demonstrated Regulatory Memory Leakage in LLM Reasoning:** The Phase 3 ablation study provides direct empirical evidence that unconstrained LLMs substitute memorized regulatory knowledge for genuine biochemical inference ([`DECISIONS.md §16 & §19`](DECISIONS.md#L160)). When forced to derive plausibility dynamically (`force_agent` mode), the LLM achieved an artificial 1.000 strict recall by explicitly citing the FDA Boxed Warning inside its biological reasoning chain, proving that dynamic LLM reasoning leaks parametric regulatory memory rather than performing de novo pathway analysis.
- **Structural Inseparability of Memorization and Clinical Reasoning:** The multi-pair memorization probe ([`DECISIONS.md §17`](DECISIONS.md#L175)) demonstrated that for any real, citable drug–event pair, parametric training memory cannot be cleanly decoupled from active clinical reasoning. This is a structural property of autoregressive foundation models that necessitates deterministic, tool-gated architectures (`lookup_first`) rather than unconstrained generative reasoning.
- **Measurable Value of Empirical Tool-Grounding:** The direct comparison against a single-shot LLM baseline on *liraglutide::pancreatic_cancer* provides concrete proof of grounding value ([`DECISIONS.md §12–13`](DECISIONS.md#L125)). The baseline hallucinated an `ESCALATE` decision (confidence 0.850) by recalling the 2013 regulatory investigation, whereas PharmaGuard queried live openFDA FAERS data, detected zero co-occurrences, and fired the Hard Safety Gate to return `DO_NOT_ESCALATE`.
- **Evaluated ReAct Execution vs. Fixed Pipeline:** In both orchestrator modes (`react_agent.py` and `fixed_pipeline.py`), official triage escalation and confidence fields are computed identically by construction via the deterministic weighted formula applied to retrieved tool evidence (Strict Precision 1.000, Strict Recall 0.857, Lenient Recall 1.000, Over-Caution Rate 12.5%). When the ReAct agent's own freeform synthesized recommendations are independently extracted and normalized, they agreed with the deterministic decisions in 11/15 benchmark cases (73.3%), with the 4 disagreements reflecting instances where unconstrained LLM synthesis leaned more aggressive or cautious than the deterministic safety gates ([`DECISIONS.md §24`](DECISIONS.md#L277)). The tool-call sequence was identical and non-varying across every pair tested, with a single synthesis step following tool execution rather than interleaved adaptive reasoning between calls; this confirms reliable, tool-grounded execution, not demonstrated adaptive agentic behavior. That distinction is noted as a known limitation, not claimed as tested.
- **Leave-One-Out (LOO) Benchmark Stability:** Systematic 15-fold single-pair perturbation across the frozen benchmark demonstrates that overall performance does not hinge on any single idiosyncratic pair ([`DECISIONS.md §29`](DECISIONS.md#L437)). Mean cross-validation performance yielded Strict $F_1 = 0.9226 \pm 0.0225$ (range: 0.9091–1.000) and Lenient $F_1 = 0.9330 \pm 0.0192$ (range: 0.9231–1.000), with narrow standard deviations verifying baseline stability ($F_1 = 0.9231$ strict, $0.9333$ lenient). The two documented edge cases account for the entire metric variance: omitting the sole strict false negative (*montelukast::suicidal_ideation*) produces a maximum strict $F_1$ swing of $+0.0769$ to 1.000, while omitting the sole lenient false positive (*metformin::hypoglycaemia*) produces a maximum lenient $F_1$ swing of $+0.0667$ to 1.000. This cross-validation operates deterministically on frozen outputs without re-executing LLM calls, meaning it tests metric sensitivity to dataset composition rather than stochastic model variance across runs.
- **Deterministic Cross-Source Evidence Agreement Heuristic:** A non-compensatory concordance metric classifies each evaluation pair as `CONCORDANT` or `DISCORDANT` based on the spread across the three normalized sub-scores ($\max \ge 0.66 \land \min \le 0.33$), flagging pairs where high-confidence evidence in one modality directly conflicts with absence of evidence in another ([`DECISIONS.md §26`](DECISIONS.md#L337)). A retroactive audit across the 15 benchmark pairs revealed that exactly 3 of 15 pairs (20.0%) are `DISCORDANT`: *montelukast::suicidal_ideation* (Grade A literature & moderate FAERS vs. unconfirmed receptor CNS mechanism), *metformin::hypoglycaemia* (strong confounded FAERS reporting vs. Grade C literature & zero mechanistic plausibility), and *atorvastatin::dementia* (associative literature debate vs. zero FAERS disproportionality). The remaining 12 pairs (80.0%) exhibit uniform concordance. As disclosed in §26, this threshold rule is an auditable operational heuristic rather than an empirically calibrated ROC cutoff, functioning as an automated alert for postmarketing human review.
- **Adversarial Mechanistic Leakage Critic:** An independent maker-checker critic agent (inspired by information-asymmetry verification patterns such as MARCH) evaluates generated biological plausibility rationales while completely blinded to drug names, adverse event terms, and primary agent scores ([`DECISIONS.md §27`](DECISIONS.md#L353)). In an empirical probe across 4 documented leakage cases, the critic achieved 100% detection sensitivity (4/4) on regulatory and parametric recall phrases. Most notably, for *montelukast::suicidal_ideation* from the Phase 3 ablation, the critic extracted verbatim regulatory leak phrases (`"FDA has issued a boxed warning"`, `"clinical data and post-marketing surveillance"`) and counterfactually downgraded the mechanistic-only score from MODERATE (0.50) to `LOW` (0.00). This confirms that the artificial 1.000 strict recall observed during unconstrained LLM derivation was entirely driven by parametric regulatory recall rather than valid molecular reasoning. The critic is designed strictly as an audit and flagging layer, and its output is not used to silently alter production scores in real time.
- **Confounding-Aware Signal Discounting with Epistemic Self-Probe:** An opt-in confounding evaluator addresses polypharmacy disproportionality in spontaneous reporting without modifying linear scoring weights or thresholds ([`DECISIONS.md §28`](DECISIONS.md#L386)). Applied to *metformin::hypoglycaemia*, the tool identified concomitant insulin and sulfonylurea co-prescriptions, assigning a 0.20 discount factor to the raw FAERS signal ($PRR = 10.73$, 9,345 reports) that reduced the adjusted PRR sub-score from 1.00 to 0.20 and dropped composite confidence from 0.4000 to 0.0800, successfully resolving the sole lenient false positive into `DO_NOT_ESCALATE`. However, a mandatory epistemic self-probe across 4 clinical pairs revealed that the confounding tool inherits the exact same parametric memorization pattern observed in plausibility derivation: the LLM recalls established clinical guideline regimens (e.g., standard diabetes co-medications) rather than deconvolving individual patient co-prescription rows from raw spontaneous data. Consequently, this capability is documented as an expert heuristic discounting layer rather than an independent mathematical proof of non-causality.
